This section presents the quantitative cross-reactivity specificity matrix for the six-protease
diagnostic panel. A position-specific scoring matrix (PSSM) was constructed for each protease
from confirmed cleavage-site sequences extracted from the MEROPS substrate database. Each panel
substrate motif was then scored against all six PSSMs to populate a $6 \times 6$ matrix of
normalised cleavage scores. The diagonal entry for each protease equals 1.0 (self-score); all
off-diagonal values below 0.05 are considered orthogonal, and values at or above 0.20 are
flagged as potentially cross-reactive.

\subsection{Motivation and Approach}

% PLACEHOLDER: write after analysis is complete
A multiplexed electrochemical biosensor requires that each protease substrate motif is cleaved
selectively by its target protease and not by other panel members. Even closely related proteases,
such as the three M10-family metalloproteinases (MMP14, MMP-8, MMP-9), have distinct subsite
preferences that can be quantified. The PSSM approach converts the body of experimentally
confirmed cleavage events in the MEROPS database into a per-protease residue preference profile
at each of the eight positions flanking the scissile bond (P4 through P4'), and then asks how
well each panel motif fits each profile.

\subsection{Data Source and PSSM Construction}

(file \texttt{pepunit.lib}, accessed July 2026). Records were filtered to the six MEROPS
identifiers listed in Table~\ref{tab:merops_ids}. After removing entries with non-standard
amino acids, the following training set sizes were obtained:

Each PSSM is an $8 \times 20$ log-odds matrix computed with a Laplace pseudocount of 0.5
and Swiss-Prot background amino acid frequencies as the null model. Log-odds scores are
base-2: positive values indicate enrichment, negative values indicate depletion at that
position.

\subsection{Interpretation}

% PLACEHOLDER: complete after results are available
The M10-family metalloproteinases (MMP14, MMP-8, MMP-9) share a preference for hydrophobic
residues at P1' and a loose [P/A]-X-[L/I]-[G/A] motif at P2-P1. Cross-reactivity within
this subfamily is expected and will be assessed quantitatively once PSSM scores are computed.
The S1-family serine proteases (NE, trypsin-like fecal) cleave C-terminal to small, hydrophobic
(elastase) or basic (trypsin) residues at P1 and are structurally distinct from the MMPs,
predicting low cross-reactivity across the metalloproteinase/serine proteinase boundary.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=gray!7, colframe=gray!40, boxrule=0.4pt,
  arc=2pt, left=6pt, right=6pt, top=4pt, bottom=4pt]
  \textbf{Caveats.} FAP (MER005153) has fewer than 20 confirmed cleavage entries in MEROPS;
  its PSSM is low-confidence and the FAP row should be interpreted cautiously.
  The fecal serine protease row represents a heterogeneous mixture of trypsin-like,
  chymotrypsin-like, and elastase-like activities collapsed into a single PSSM by
  pooling all three MEROPS entry sets; this introduces additional uncertainty.
  Both issues are documented quantitatively via 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals
  computed from 500 resamples of each training window set.
\end{tcolorbox}
