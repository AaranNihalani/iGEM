\noindent
Layer~0 predicts the \textbf{free protease concentrations in wound fluid at the
time of swab sampling}. Its two key outputs, $[\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free}}(t)$
and $[\text{HNE}]_{\text{free}}(t)$, are in-situ wound fluid concentrations that
pass to Layer~1 (sample collection) via a swab dilution factor $D_f$.

The electrochemical device measures these concentrations \emph{indirectly} via peptide
substrate cleavage. MMP-9 and HNE each cleave a surface-immobilised peptide probe
bearing a methylene-blue (MB) redox label; cleavage releases MB into solution,
producing a signal-off decrease in SWV peak current proportional to enzymatic activity.
The sensor therefore reports \emph{activity}, not protein concentration directly.
Layer~0 provides the free enzyme concentration that drives this activity; the kinetic
link between $[\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free}}$ and cleavage rate requires $k_{\text{cat}}$
and $K_m$ for the specific peptide substrate (Section~\ref{sec:output}, Limitation~5).

The model tracks nine coupled species through a system of ordinary differential
equations (Section~\ref{sec:ode}), solved numerically with a stiff implicit integrator.
Every parameter value must be grounded in a paper physically present in
\texttt{papers/layer\_00/}; values that are estimates rather than text-confirmed
are explicitly labelled throughout.

\section{Wound Fluid Composition}
\label{sec:composition}

\subsection{Biological rationale}

Chronic wounds are characterised by a prolonged inflammatory phase sustained by
persistent neutrophil and macrophage infiltration \cit{1}. This drives elevated
secretion of two serine and metalloprotease families that are the targets of this
device. MMP-9 (gelatinase B, a zinc-dependent matrix metalloprotease) is secreted as
an inactive zymogen (pro-MMP-9); activation requires proteolytic removal of the
prodomain. HNE (human neutrophil elastase) is released already active from azurophil
granules and is regulated primarily by $\alpha_1$-antitrypsin (A1AT) and secondarily
by secretory leukocyte protease inhibitor (SLPI).

\subsection{Unit conversion}

All species concentrations are held internally in mol/L. The conversion from the
ng/mL units reported in ELISA literature is:

\begin{equation}
C \;\bigg[\frac{\text{mol}}{\text{L}}\bigg]
= \frac{c\;\bigg[\dfrac{\text{ng}}{\text{mL}}\bigg]
  \times 10^{-6}\;\bigg[\dfrac{\text{g/L}}{\text{ng/mL}}\bigg]}
  {M_W\;\bigg[\dfrac{\text{g}}{\text{mol}}\bigg]}
\label{eq:unit}
\end{equation}

For $\mu$g/mL values the prefactor is $10^{-3}$ instead of $10^{-6}$. Applying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:unit} to active MMP-9 in chronic wound fluid (4800 ng/mL,
$M_W = 92{,}000$ g/mol):

\begin{equation*}
C_{\text{MMP-9}} = \frac{4800 \times 10^{-6}}{92{,}000}
= 5.22 \times 10^{-8}\;\text{mol/L} = 52\;\text{nM}
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Literature values}

\textbf{Ladwig et~al.\ (2002)} \cit{2} used quantitative ELISA and gelatin
zymography on wound fluid from 56 patients with chronic pressure ulcers. All values
below are from paper text (p.~31):

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Pro-MMP-9: $8{,}100 \pm 3{,}000$ ng/mL; active MMP-9: $4{,}800 \pm 1{,}300$ ng/mL.
  \item Total MMP-9 $= 12{,}900$ ng/mL; pro-form fraction $= 63\%$.
  \item MMP-9:TIMP-1 ratio inversely correlated with healing
    (text pp.~33--34): higher active MMP-9 and lower TIMP-1 in non-healing wounds.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Trengove et~al.\ (1999)} \cit{1} used ELISA in venous leg ulcer (VLU) wound
fluid. Values from text (p.~448):

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item TIMP-1 in chronic VLU: median 0.8~$\mu$g/mL $= 800$ ng/mL ($n = 5$).
  \item HNE in non-healing VLU: median 1.4~$\mu$g/mL $\approx 47$ nM
    (range $<1$--11.9~$\mu$g/mL; elevated in 6 of 13 chronic samples).
  \item MMP activity significantly lower in healing wounds ($p < 0.01$, text p.~449).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Rao et~al.\ (1995)} \cit{7} examined A1AT integrity by western blot and
elastase-inhibitory capacity (text pp.~572--575):

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item A1AT intact and functional in all healing and acute wound samples.
  \item A1AT degraded and non-functional in 9 of 10 chronic wound samples.
  \item No quantitative ng/mL wound-fluid A1AT concentration is given in Rao~1995.
    The healing-state estimate $[A_0] \approx 1.92\;\mu\text{M}$ (serum
    $\approx 2$~mg/mL at 10-fold dilution into wound fluid) is used; Section~\ref{sec:hne}
    shows the qualitative conclusion is insensitive to this estimate.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Nwomeh et~al.\ (1999)} \cit{8} reported that TIMP-1 was ``very low or not
detected'' in chronic ulcer fluid (text p.~193), consistent with Trengove~1999~\cit{1}
and confirming TIMP-1 suppression as a hallmark of the chronic wound environment.
Values are expressed per~$\mu$g protein and cannot be converted to ng/mL without the
protein content data, so Trengove~1999 is used for quantitative TIMP-1.

\section{MMP-9 Inhibition by TIMP-1}
\label{sec:mmp9}

TIMP-1 binds reversibly to the MMP-9 active site in non-covalent slow-tight inhibition.
Denoting free MMP-9 as $M$, free TIMP-1 as $T$, and their complex as $C_1$, the
rate equations are:

\begin{align}
\frac{d[M]}{dt} &= -\kon^T\,[M][T] + \koff^T\,[C_1] \label{eq:dM} \\
\frac{d[T]}{dt} &= -\kon^T\,[M][T] + \koff^T\,[C_1] \label{eq:dT} \\
\frac{d[C_1]}{dt} &= +\kon^T\,[M][T] - \koff^T\,[C_1] \label{eq:dC1}
\end{align}

Olson et~al.\ (1997)~\cit{4} reported kinetic constants from solution-phase enzyme
inhibition assays (Table~IV, 82~kDa active MMP-9):

\begin{align*}
\kon^T &= 2.48 \pm 0.47 \times 10^5\;\Mms \\
\koff^T &= 2.1 \pm 1.1 \times 10^{-3}\;\text{s}^{-1}
\end{align*}

The equilibrium inhibition constant $K_i$ is stated directly in Olson~1997 Table~IV
as $K_i = 8.5 \pm 2.9$ nM; the ratio confirms this:

\begin{equation}
K_i = \frac{\koff^T}{\kon^T}
= \frac{2.1 \times 10^{-3}}{2.48 \times 10^5}
= 8.47\;\text{nM} \approx 8.5\;\text{nM}
\label{eq:Ki}
\end{equation}

Enzyme inhibition values are used in preference to the SPR values in Olson~1997
Tables~I and~II because solution-phase kinetics are more representative of wound-fluid
conditions; $\kon^T$ from enzyme inhibition is 2.5--7$\times$ higher than from SPR
(Olson~1997, p.~29981).

\subsection{Equilibrium free MMP-9}

The TIMP-1/MMP-9 equilibration timescale is:

\begin{equation*}
\tau = \frac{1}{\kon^T\,[T_0]}
= \frac{1}{2.48 \times 10^5 \times 28 \times 10^{-9}}
\approx 144\;\text{s} \approx 2.4\;\text{min}
\end{equation*}

This is short relative to the 30-min measurement window, so the system rapidly
reaches an approximate binding equilibrium. Setting $d[C_1]/dt = 0$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dC1} gives:

\begin{equation}
K_i = \frac{[M][T]}{[C_1]} \label{eq:Keq}
\end{equation}

Conservation of each species pool requires:

\begin{align}
[M_0] &= [M] + [C_1] \label{eq:Mcons} \\
[T_0] &= [T] + [C_1] \label{eq:Tcons}
\end{align}

From Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mcons}: $[C_1] = [M_0] - [M]$. Substituting into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Tcons}: $[T] = [T_0] - [M_0] + [M]$. Substituting both into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Keq} and writing $x = [M]_{\text{free}}$:

\begin{equation*}
K_i\,(M_0 - x) = x\,(T_0 - M_0 + x)
\end{equation*}

Expanding and collecting terms:

\begin{equation}
x^2 + x\,(T_0 - M_0 + K_i) - K_i\,M_0 = 0
\label{eq:quadratic}
\end{equation}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:quadratic} is a quadratic in $x$. Taking the positive root (the only
physically meaningful solution, since $[M]_{\text{free}} \geq 0$):

\begin{equation}
[M]_{\text{free}} =
\frac{-(T_0 - M_0 + K_i) + \sqrt{(T_0 - M_0 + K_i)^2 + 4\,K_i\,M_0}}{2}
\label{eq:Mfree}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Chronic wound} ($M_0 = 52$ nM, $T_0 = 28$ nM, $K_i = 8.5$ nM):

\begin{align*}
b &= T_0 - M_0 + K_i = 28 - 52 + 8.5 = -15.5\;\text{nM} \\
[M]_{\text{free}} &=
  \frac{15.5 + \sqrt{(-15.5)^2 + 4 \times 8.5 \times 52}}{2} \\
  &= \frac{15.5 + \sqrt{240.3 + 1768}}{2}
   = \frac{15.5 + 44.8}{2}
   \approx \mathbf{30.2\;\text{nM}}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Healing wound} ($M_0 = 22$ nM, $T_0 = 39$ nM, $K_i = 8.5$ nM):

\begin{align*}
b &= T_0 - M_0 + K_i = 39 - 22 + 8.5 = +25.5\;\text{nM} \\
[M]_{\text{free}} &=
  \frac{-25.5 + \sqrt{(25.5)^2 + 4 \times 8.5 \times 22}}{2} \\
  &= \frac{-25.5 + \sqrt{650.3 + 748}}{2}
   = \frac{-25.5 + 37.4}{2}
   \approx \mathbf{5.9\;\text{nM}}
\end{align*}

These analytical equilibrium values agree closely with the full ODE result at
$t = 30$ min (31.9 nM chronic, 6.2 nM healing; Table~\ref{tab:outputs}). The small
difference reflects ongoing pro-MMP-9 activation driving the system slightly away
from the pure TIMP-1 equilibrium.

\subsection{Sensitivity to $K_i$}

Changing $K_i$ from 8.5 nM to 1 nM in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mfree} shifts the chronic
result from 30.2 nM to 26.5 nM (a 12\% decrease). The healing result is more
sensitive: at $K_i = 1$ nM it falls from 5.9 nM to 1.4 nM, because the TIMP-1
excess (17 nM) is comparable to $K_i$. The reported uncertainty ($\pm 2.9$ nM) is
within this regime; the healing estimate should be treated as order-of-magnitude.

The following assumptions underlie this section:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item The equilibrium approximation is valid because $\tau \approx 2.4$ min is
    short relative to the 30-min read window and the pro-MMP-9 activation timescale.
  \item Solution-phase enzyme inhibition constants (Olson~1997 \cit{4} Table~IV)
    are used in preference to SPR values, as the wound fluid environment is closer to
    solution phase.
  \item Pro-MMP-9 activation (Section~\ref{sec:ode}) is negligible with current
    placeholder kinetics; the active MMP-9 initial condition is approximately constant
    over the measurement window.
\end{enumerate}

\section{HNE Inhibition}
\label{sec:hne}

\subsection{A1AT/HNE near-irreversible binding}

A1AT inhibits HNE via serpin mechanism: a covalent acyl-enzyme intermediate forms
and the complex is effectively irreversible ($\koff^A \approx 0$). The rate of free
HNE depletion is:

\begin{equation}
\frac{d[H]}{dt} = -\kon^A\,[A][H]
\label{eq:dH}
\end{equation}

In healing wounds where $[A_0] \gg [H_0]$ (see below), the free A1AT concentration
remains approximately constant throughout the reaction. This pseudo-first-order
approximation reduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:dH} to:

\begin{equation}
\frac{d[H]}{dt} \approx -k^*\,[H], \qquad k^* = \kon^A\,[A_0]
\label{eq:pseudo}
\end{equation}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:pseudo} is a first-order linear ODE with exponential solution:

\begin{equation}
[H](t) = [H_0]\,\exp\!\bigl(-\kon^A\,[A_0]\,t\bigr)
\label{eq:Hdecay}
\end{equation}

The half-life, at which $[H] = [H_0]/2$, follows from setting $\exp(-k^* t_{1/2}) = 1/2$:

\begin{equation}
t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\kon^A\cdot[A_0]}
\label{eq:thalf}
\end{equation}

Beatty, Bieth \& Travis (1980)~\cit{5} confirmed $\kon^A = 6.5 \pm 4.0 \times 10^7\;\Mms$
from direct kinetic measurement (Table~1, human leukocyte elastase vs.\ native
$\alpha_1$-PI). With the healing-wound estimate $[A_0] = 1.92\;\mu\text{M}$:

\begin{equation}
t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{6.5 \times 10^7 \times 1.92 \times 10^{-6}}
= \frac{0.693}{124.8}
\approx 5.5\;\text{ms}
\label{eq:thalf_val}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Sensitivity to $[A_0]$.}
At $[A_0]$ ten-fold lower ($0.192\;\mu$M):

\begin{equation}
t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{6.5 \times 10^7 \times 0.192 \times 10^{-6}} \approx 55\;\text{ms}
\label{eq:thalf_lo}
\end{equation}

Even at this lower bound, $t_{1/2} = 55$ ms remains three orders of magnitude below
the 5-min practical minimum measurement window. The healing-wound conclusion,
$[\text{HNE}]_{\text{free}} \approx 0$, is therefore insensitive to the [A1AT]
estimate over a 10-fold range.

\textit{Validity check.} In healing wounds $[A_0] = 1.92\;\mu\text{M} \gg [H_0] = 47$ nM,
confirming the pseudo-first-order approximation. After complete HNE absorption:
$[A]_{\text{free}} \approx 1.92\;\mu\text{M} - 47\;\text{nM} \approx 1.87\;\mu\text{M}$
(confirmed by ODE, Table~\ref{tab:outputs}).

\subsection{A1AT wound-state dependence}

Rao et~al.\ (1995)~\cit{7} showed that functional A1AT cannot be assumed present
across wound types:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Healing and acute wounds}: A1AT intact and functional in all samples.
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:thalf_val} applies; $[\text{HNE}]_{\text{free}} = 0$ within milliseconds.
  \item \textbf{Chronic wounds}: A1AT degraded and non-functional in 9 of 10 samples.
    With functional $[A_0] \approx 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dH} gives $d[H]/dt \approx 0$:
    HNE persists in wound fluid at near its initial concentration (moderated only by SLPI).
\end{itemize}

This A1AT wound-state switch provides a second discriminating axis: healing wounds have
$[\text{HNE}]_{\text{free}} \approx 0$ while chronic wounds (where A1AT is degraded)
retain elevated free HNE. However, HNE elevation itself was detected in only 6 of 13
chronic VLU samples in Trengove~1999~\cit{1}, meaning $\sim$50\% of chronic cases are
HNE-low regardless of A1AT status. HNE is therefore not a reliable standalone
discriminator (see also Section~\ref{sec:output}).

\subsection{SLPI secondary inhibition in chronic wounds}

In chronic wounds where A1AT is non-functional, SLPI provides the residual HNE
inhibition. Ashcroft et~al.\ (2000)~\cit{6} confirmed SLPI's role in wound healing
using knockout mice but did not report a quantitative wound-fluid SLPI concentration.
The model uses placeholder kinetics ($\kon^S = 10^5\;\Mms$, $\koff^S = 10^{-4}$ s$^{-1}$,
$[S_0] \approx 200$ ng/mL $\approx 17$ nM); with these values the ODE gives
$[\text{HNE}]_{\text{free}} \approx 30$ nM at $t = 30$ min in chronic wounds.
This prediction is sensitive to the placeholder SLPI kinetics.

The following assumptions underlie this section:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item Pseudo-first-order kinetics are valid for healing wounds because
    $[A_0] = 1.92\;\mu\text{M} \gg [H_0] = 47$ nM.
  \item $[A_0] = 1.92\;\mu\text{M}$ is a serum/10 estimate; the half-life
    conclusion is insensitive over a 10-fold range (Eq.~\eqref{eq:thalf_lo}).
  \item $\kon^A$ was measured at 25$^{\circ}$C, pH~8.0 in Beatty~1980~\cit{5};
    applied here at wound pH~7.4 as the best available value.
  \item SLPI concentration and kinetics are unconfirmed placeholders; the chronic
    HNE prediction is not considered quantitatively reliable.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Full ODE System}
\label{sec:ode}

\subsection{Species and notation}

Nine coupled species track the wound fluid protease cascade. All concentrations in mol/L:

\begin{center}
\small\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{\quad}ll@{}}
$P$ & pro-MMP-9         & $C_1$ & MMP-9:TIMP-1 complex \\
$M$ & free (active) MMP-9 & $H$   & free HNE \\
$T$ & free TIMP-1        & $A$   & free A1AT \\
$C_2$ & HNE:A1AT complex & $S$   & free SLPI \\
$C_3$ & HNE:SLPI complex &       & \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Governing equations}

Pro-MMP-9 is activated by MMP-3 and plasmin in excess, so the activation is
pseudo-first-order in $P$ with effective rate $k^{**}$:

\begin{align}
\dot{P}   &= -k^{**}\,P \label{eq:dP} \\
\dot{M}   &= +k^{**}\,P - \kon^T MT + \koff^T C_1 \label{eq:dM2} \\
\dot{T}   &= -\kon^T MT + \koff^T C_1 \label{eq:dT2} \\
\dot{C}_1 &= +\kon^T MT - \koff^T C_1 \label{eq:dC12} \\
\dot{H}   &= -\kon^A HA - \kon^S HS + \koff^S C_3 \label{eq:dH2} \\
\dot{A}   &= -\kon^A HA \label{eq:dA} \\
\dot{C}_2 &= +\kon^A HA \label{eq:dC2} \\
\dot{S}   &= -\kon^S HS + \koff^S C_3 \label{eq:dS} \\
\dot{C}_3 &= +\kon^S HS - \koff^S C_3 \label{eq:dC3}
\end{align}

where $k^{**} = (k_{\text{cat}}/K_m)_{\text{MMP-3}}\,[\text{MMP-3}]
+ (k_{\text{cat}}/K_m)_{\text{plasmin}}\,[\text{plasmin}]$ is the total first-order
activation rate; $\kon^T$, $\koff^T$ from Olson~1997~\cit{4};
$\kon^A$ from Beatty~1980~\cit{5}; $\kon^S$, $\koff^S$ are placeholders.

\subsection{Mass conservation}

Adding the appropriate equations confirms that each species pool is separately conserved:

\begin{align*}
\tfrac{d}{dt}(P + M + C_1)    &= 0 \qquad \text{(total MMP-9)} \\
\tfrac{d}{dt}(T + C_1)        &= 0 \qquad \text{(total TIMP-1)} \\
\tfrac{d}{dt}(H + C_2 + C_3)  &= 0 \qquad \text{(total HNE)} \\
\tfrac{d}{dt}(A + C_2)        &= 0 \qquad \text{(total A1AT)} \\
\tfrac{d}{dt}(S + C_3)        &= 0 \qquad \text{(total SLPI)}
\end{align*}

These identities provide a numerical check: deviations indicate integration error.
The Radau solver maintains conservation to within
$\text{rtol} = 10^{-10}$, $\text{atol} = 10^{-15}$ tolerances.

\subsection{Stiff integration}

The system spans seven orders of magnitude in characteristic timescale:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item A1AT/HNE reaction: $t_{1/2} \approx 5.5$ ms (fastest)
  \item TIMP-1/MMP-9 equilibration: $\tau \approx 2.4$ min (intermediate)
  \item Pro-MMP-9 activation: $\sim$120 min (slowest; negligible with placeholders)
\end{itemize}

The timescale ratio $\sim$$10^7$ makes the system stiff: explicit solvers would require
$\Delta t \lesssim 1$ ms, making a 120-min integration prohibitively expensive.
The Radau implicit solver (\texttt{scipy.integrate.solve\_ivp}, method~\texttt{Radau})
is used throughout.

\subsection{MMP-9 wound-state discrimination}

The 5.1$\times$ discrimination ratio arises from the sign of the MMP-9 vs.\ TIMP-1
excess, as made explicit by Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mfree}:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Chronic wound}: MMP-9 (52 nM) exceeds TIMP-1 (28 nM) by 24 nM.
    With $K_i = 8.5$ nM $\ll M_0, T_0$, TIMP-1 is nearly fully bound and free
    MMP-9 persists at $\approx 32$ nM.
  \item \textbf{Healing wound} (estimated): TIMP-1 (39 nM) exceeds MMP-9 (22 nM)
    by 17 nM. TIMP-1 excess suppresses free MMP-9 to $\approx 6$ nM.
\end{itemize}

The mechanism is structurally robust: for $K_i \ll M_0, T_0$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mfree} gives $[M]_{\text{free}} \approx M_0 - T_0$ to first order
(the excess simply remains free). Any wound state where active MMP-9 exceeds TIMP-1
will show elevated free MMP-9, independent of exact parameter values.

\subsection{HNE wound-state discrimination}

The A1AT wound-state switch (Rao~1995~\cit{7}, Section~\ref{sec:hne}) produces a
strong binary in HNE: healing wounds have $[\text{HNE}]_{\text{free}} = 0.00$ pM,
while chronic wounds with degraded A1AT show $[\text{HNE}]_{\text{free}} \approx 30$ nM.
However, elevated HNE was observed in only 6/13 (50\%) of chronic VLU samples
(Trengove~1999~\cit{1}); the remaining 50\% of chronic wounds are HNE-low regardless
of A1AT status. HNE is not a reliable primary discriminant; MMP-9 ($5\times$
discrimination, grounded in confirmed kinetics) is the preferred target. The second
electrode targeting HNE serves as an internal reference rather than a primary channel.

\subsection{Cleavage kinetics and sensor signal}

The sensor signal is the fractional SWV current decrease:

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Delta I}{I_0} = \frac{I_0 - I(t)}{I_0} = f_{\text{cleaved}}(t)
  \label{eq:signal_off}
\end{equation}

where $I_0$ is the pre-incubation baseline current (all MB tethered) and $I(t)$ is the
current at assay time $t$ after wound fluid exposure. Both measurements are made in the
same buffer, so any Ca$^{2+}$-induced change in MB-electrode electron transfer rate
applies equally to $I_0$ and $I(t)$ and cancels in the ratio (Layer~7).

The cleavage velocity per unit electrode area at the start of the assay is:

\begin{equation}
  v = k_{\text{cat}} \cdot [\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free}} \cdot
      \frac{\Gamma_p}{K_m^{\text{eff}} + \Gamma_p}
  \label{eq:mm}
\end{equation}

where $\Gamma_p$ is the probe surface density (from Layer~7) and $K_m^{\text{eff}}$ is
the apparent Michaelis constant for surface-immobilised substrate. In the linear regime
($\Gamma_p \ll K_m^{\text{eff}}$):

\begin{equation}
  v \approx \frac{k_{\text{cat}}}{K_m} \cdot [\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free}} \cdot \Gamma_p
  \label{eq:mm_linear}
\end{equation}

The wound-state discrimination ratio for sensor signal equals the ratio of cleavage
velocities. In the linear regime, $k_{\text{cat}}$, $K_m$, and $\Gamma_p$ cancel:

\begin{equation}
  \frac{v_{\text{chronic}}}{v_{\text{healing}}}
  = \frac{[\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free,chronic}}}{[\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free,healing}}}
  \approx \frac{30\ \text{nM}}{6\ \text{nM}}
  \approx 5\times
  \label{eq:activity_ratio}
\end{equation}

If TIMP-1 inhibition is competitive with the peptide substrate, the apparent inhibition
constant is modified by substrate occupancy:

\begin{equation}
  K_{i,\text{app}} = K_i\!\left(1 + \frac{[S]}{K_m}\right)
  \label{eq:kiapp}
\end{equation}

where $[S]/K_m$ is the fractional saturation of the enzyme by substrate. In the linear
regime ($[S] \ll K_m$), $K_{i,\text{app}} \approx K_i = 8.5$~nM and the ODE
equilibrium is self-consistent. At higher substrate occupancy, TIMP-1 inhibition weakens
($K_{i,\text{app}} > K_i$), which would increase free MMP-9 in healing wounds relative
to the ODE prediction and potentially reduce the discrimination ratio below $5\times$.

Kridel et~al.\ (2001)~\cit{9} (Table~II) measured kinetic parameters for MMP-9 cleaving
peptide A11 (SGKIPRTLTA), the device substrate sequence (50~mM Tris, pH~7.5, 10~mM
CaCl$_2$, 37$^\circ$C):
\begin{equation}
  k_{\text{cat}} = 86.4\ \text{s}^{-1},\quad K_m = 1.3\ \text{mM},\quad
  \frac{k_{\text{cat}}}{K_m} = 67{,}500\ \Mms
  \label{eq:kinetics_a11}
\end{equation}
The substrate competition correction at the electrode surface is discussed in Limitation~5.

\subsection{Layer~1 dilution}

The required dilution factor for the device entry concentration to fall within the
enzyme activity detection window is established in Layer~1 using $k_{\text{cat}}$,
$K_m$, and the signal sensitivity (LOD in terms of $\Delta I/I_0$). The solution-phase
kinetic constants ($k_{\text{cat}} = 86.4\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $K_m = 1.3\ \text{mM}$)
are now confirmed from Kridel~2001~\cit{9}. The remaining blocker is the surface
effective rate constant $k_{\text{eff,surf}}$, which must be measured on the fabricated
electrode (Layer~9) before the LOD and dilution factor can be specified quantitatively.
The in-situ free MMP-9 concentrations from Table~\ref{tab:outputs} (30~nM chronic,
6~nM healing) are the inputs to the Layer~1 calculation.

\subsection{Limitations of this model}

Each limitation below states what is simplified, explains why the simplification does not
invalidate the model's primary output (the MMP-9 wound-state discrimination ratio), and
identifies what experiment would resolve it.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]

  \item \textbf{Healing wound initial conditions are not text-confirmed.}
    The chronic wound values are the primary output of this model: active MMP-9 = 52~nM
    and TIMP-1 = 28~nM are both text-confirmed from Ladwig~2002 (p.~31)~\cit{2}, and
    all kinetic parameters ($K_i$, $\kon^T$, $\koff^T$) are text-confirmed from
    Olson~1997 Table~IV~\cit{4}. The healing MMP-9 estimate (5,400~ng/mL) is read from
    Ladwig~2002 Figure~8; the healing TIMP-1 (1,104~ng/mL) has no paper source.
    These estimates are used only to compute a comparison point, not to determine the
    chronic wound output. The model's structural conclusion, that any wound state in
    which active MMP-9 exceeds TIMP-1 will yield elevated free MMP-9 (from the sign
    of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mfree}), is independent of the healing estimates. The 5$\times$
    ratio should be treated as a working figure. Resolution requires a joint VLU dataset
    reporting both MMP-9 and TIMP-1 from the same patients at chronic and healing
    time points.

  \item \textbf{SLPI kinetics are unconfirmed placeholders and do not affect MMP-9.}
    The SLPI sub-system (Eqs.~\ref{eq:dS}--\ref{eq:dC3}) is completely decoupled from
    the MMP-9/TIMP-1 sub-system: SLPI does not bind MMP-9 or TIMP-1 and cannot
    alter the MMP-9 partition calculated in Section~\ref{sec:mmp9}. SLPI uncertainty
    propagates exclusively to the HNE secondary channel, which is already acknowledged
    as a limited discriminant (Limitation~4 below). The SLPI concentration and kinetics
    ($\kon^S = 10^5\;\Mms$, $\koff^S = 10^{-4}$~s$^{-1}$) are order-of-magnitude
    placeholders; Ashcroft~2000~\cit{6} confirms SLPI's role in wound healing from
    knockout mice but provides no quantitative wound-fluid SLPI concentration.
    Wound-fluid SLPI ELISA would resolve this for the HNE channel.

  \item \textbf{Pro-MMP-9 activation is negligible at current parameter values.}
    The active MMP-9 initial condition (52~nM, chronic; 22~nM, healing) is taken
    directly from Ladwig~2002~\cit{2} and does not depend on the activation sub-model.
    The activation kinetics (via MMP-3 and plasmin; Eq.~\ref{eq:dP}) use
    order-of-magnitude placeholders, and with these values less than 1\% of pro-MMP-9
    converts in 120~min. This means the active MMP-9 pool is effectively constant over
    the 30-minute read window, consistent with using the Ladwig ELISA snapshot as the
    initial condition. These activation parameters become important only if wound-fluid
    MMP-3 or plasmin concentrations prove substantially higher than assumed, which would
    require a separate measurement.

  \item \textbf{HNE elevation is inconsistent across chronic wound samples.}
    Elevated free HNE was detected in only 6 of 13 chronic VLU samples in
    Trengove~1999~\cit{1}: 50\% of chronic wounds are HNE-low regardless of A1AT
    status. The HNE channel therefore has limited diagnostic utility and is not the
    primary discriminant. This limitation does not affect MMP-9, which shows elevated
    free concentration in all chronic wound samples by the TIMP-1/MMP-9 excess
    mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:mmp9}). The HNE electrode is treated as a secondary
    reference channel, not a primary diagnostic output.

  \item \textbf{$k_{\text{cat}}$ and $K_m$ for SGKIPRTLTA are now confirmed from
    Kridel et~al.\ (2001)~\cit{9}; the substrate competition correction at the electrode
    surface must be quantified.}

    Kridel et~al.\ (2001)~\cit{9} Table~II reports solution-phase kinetics for MMP-9
    cleaving peptide A11 (SGKIPRTLTA):
    \begin{equation*}
      k_{\text{cat}} = 86.4\ \text{s}^{-1},\quad K_m = 1.3\ \text{mM},\quad
      k_{\text{cat}}/K_m = 67{,}500\ \Mms
    \end{equation*}
    measured by fluorescamine incorporation assay at pH~7.5, 10~mM CaCl$_2$, 37$^\circ$C.
    These are solution-phase values; surface immobilisation systematically reduces
    $k_{\text{cat}}/K_m$ (Layer~9), so they serve as an upper bound for the surface
    effective rate constant $k_{\text{eff,surf}}$.

    \textbf{Wound fluid equilibrium.} The Layer~0 ODE models the TIMP-1/MMP-9 partition
    in \emph{wound fluid}, not at the electrode. TIMP-1 equilibrates with MMP-9 in the
    wound fluid bulk, where the relevant competing substrates are endogenous (collagen,
    gelatin) at concentrations that do not enter the ODE. The electrode peptide at
    $\Gamma_p \sim 3\ \text{pmol~cm}^{-2}$ represents sub-nanomolar total moles in the
    assay volume and is a negligible perturbation on the wound fluid equilibrium. The
    ODE $K_i = 8.5$~nM (Olson~1997~\cit{4}) therefore remains valid for the wound
    fluid partition.

    \textbf{Justification for sequential treatment.} A fully coupled competitive-inhibition
    scheme would require solving TIMP-1 inhibition and surface Michaelis--Menten kinetics
    simultaneously, since TIMP-1 and the surface peptide compete for the MMP-9 active site.
    The sequential approach used here, in which the wound fluid ODE is solved first to obtain
    $[\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free}}$ and that value is then used as a constant input to the
    surface MM velocity, is valid under two conditions that are both satisfied.
    First, the wound fluid bulk contains no surface peptide substrate
    ($[S]_{\text{bulk}} \approx 0$), so the ODE inhibition equilibrium uses $K_i = 8.5$~nM
    without any substrate competition term, and the result is internally self-consistent.
    Second, the $[\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free}}$ obtained from the wound fluid ODE is a
    \emph{conservative lower bound} on electrode-accessible enzyme: at the electrode surface
    $K_{i,\text{app}} \approx 33$~nM (Eq.~\ref{eq:kiapp_calc}), meaning TIMP-1 inhibition
    is weaker at the electrode than in the wound fluid. The sequential treatment therefore
    under-predicts, not over-predicts, the enzyme available for surface cleavage, and the
    discrimination ratio of $5\times$ is conservatively estimated.

    \textbf{Substrate depletion is negligible.} The total surface-immobilised SGKIPRTLTA on
    the electrode is $\Gamma_p \times A \approx 5\ \text{pmol~cm}^{-2} \times
    0.002\ \text{cm}^2 = 10\ \text{fmol}$. The bulk MMP-9 in the assay volume is
    $\sim\!0.1$--$0.2\ \text{pmol}$ (at 5--10~nM in $\sim\!20\ \mu\text{L}$). Even
    if all surface substrate were cleaved simultaneously, that represents
    $<0.1\%$ of the total MMP-9 pool. Substrate depletion at this level cannot perturb
    the TIMP-1/MMP-9 binding equilibrium ($K_i = 8.5$~nM, Olson~1997~\cit{4}), which
    requires a $\gtrsim$10\% perturbation in free MMP-9 to shift the partition
    measurably. Consequently, $[\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free}}$ from the wound fluid ODE
    remains accurate throughout the Layer~9 assay window, and no TIMP-1 competitive
    inhibition correction term is required in the Layer~9 $k_{\text{eff}}$ expression.

    \textbf{Electrode surface correction.} At the electrode surface the effective local
    substrate concentration depends on probe density and probe-layer thickness. For
    $\Gamma_p = 3\ \text{pmol~cm}^{-2}$ and effective layer thickness
    $d_{\text{eff}} \approx 8\ \text{nm}$ (Layer~8):
    \begin{equation}
      [S]_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\Gamma_p}{d_{\text{eff}}}
      = \frac{3 \times 10^{-12}\ \text{mol~cm}^{-2}}{8 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{cm}}
      \approx 3.75\ \text{mM}
      \label{eq:Seff}
    \end{equation}
    Since $[S]_{\text{eff}}/K_m \approx 3.75/1.3 \approx 2.9$, the electrode surface
    is not in the linear regime. The Michaelis--Menten velocity is:
    \begin{equation}
      v = k_{\text{cat}} \cdot [\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{free}} \cdot
          \frac{\Gamma_p}{K_m + [S]_{\text{eff}}}
      \label{eq:mm_nonlinear}
    \end{equation}
    The wound-state discrimination ratio $v_{\text{chronic}}/v_{\text{healing}}$ still
    reduces to $[\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{chronic}}/[\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{healing}} \approx
    5\times$ because $K_m$ and $[S]_{\text{eff}}$ cancel identically in the ratio.
    The non-linear regime therefore does not reduce the discrimination ratio but does
    affect the absolute signal magnitude and the apparent TIMP-1 inhibition. If TIMP-1
    inhibition is competitive with the surface peptide, the apparent inhibition constant
    at the electrode is:
    \begin{equation}
      K_{i,\text{app}} = K_i\!\left(1 + \frac{[S]_{\text{eff}}}{K_m}\right)
      \approx 8.5 \times (1 + 2.9) \approx 33\ \text{nM}
      \label{eq:kiapp_calc}
    \end{equation}
    This weakens apparent TIMP-1 inhibition at the electrode surface, increasing the
    fraction of MMP-9 available for cleavage relative to the wound fluid ODE prediction.
    The surface $k_{\text{cat}}/K_m$ must be measured directly on the fabricated
    electrode (Layer~9) before the absolute signal magnitude can be specified.

    For HNE: A1AT inhibition is a serpin acyl-enzyme mechanism, not competitive inhibition.
    There is no $K_{i,\text{app}}$ correction. The half-life conclusion is insensitive to a
    10-fold change in $\kon^A$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:thalf_lo}). The $k_{\text{cat}}$ and $K_m$
    for HNE cleaving the HNE peptide substrate are not yet confirmed from any paper in
    \texttt{papers/layer\_00/} and remain an outstanding parameter for the HNE channel.

\end{enumerate}
