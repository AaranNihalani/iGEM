\noindent
The SWV forward model answers a deceptively simple question: given that a fraction
$\Fk$ of the peptide probes on the electrode surface have been cleaved by MMP-9,
what net voltammogram would the square-wave voltammeter actually record? Answering
this question requires chaining together three separate physical models, each
confirmed by independent experimental literature, into a single quantitative map
$\Fk \mapsto \DI(E)$.

The three components are: (i) a tether geometry model that determines the probability
distribution $P(d)$ of methylene blue (MB) label distances from the gold surface; (ii)
an electron-transfer (ET) kinetics model that converts each distance $d$ into a
rate constant $\kET(d)$; and (iii) a Butler--Volmer square-wave voltammetry (BV-SWV)
simulator that converts $\kET$ into a net voltammogram $\DI(E)$. Cleavage enters
multiplicatively: a probe that loses its MB label makes no contribution to any of
these steps.

All parameter values are confirmed from papers physically present in
\texttt{papers/swv\_forward\_model/} or cross-referenced from other layer paper
folders as noted. Values that remain unconfirmed are labelled \FLAG.

\section{Pipeline Context and Motivation}
\label{sec:pipeline}

\subsection{Where this model sits}

The 17-layer biosensor pipeline progresses from wound biology (Layer~0) through
sample collection and dilution (Layers~1--5) to the surface bioassay (Layers~6--9)
and electrochemical readout (Layers~10--14). The SWV forward model synthesises six
of these layers:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Layer~6} (probe surface density): $\Gtot = 3\times10^{-12}$ mol\,cm$^{-2}$,
    set by Lubin 2010 mixed SAM protocol \cit{5}.
  \item \textbf{Layer~8} (steric correction): surface-phase $k_{\text{eff}}$ relative to
    solution $k_{\text{cat}}/K_m$; sets the cleavage kinetics that produce $\Fk(t)$.
  \item \textbf{Layer~9} (cleavage kinetics): MMP-9 cleaves SGKIPRTLTA with
    $k_{\text{cat}}/K_m = 67{,}500$ M$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$ (Kridel 2001 \cit{4}).
  \item \textbf{Layer~10} (tether geometry, ET decay): $P(d)$ and $\kET(d)$ from FJC
    model and Smalley 1995 \cit{3}.
  \item \textbf{Layer~11} (SWV waveform): BV-SWV ODE from O'Dea~1981 \cit{6}
    and Dauphin-Ducharme 2017 \cit{1}.
  \item \textbf{Layer~13} (ratiometric normalisation): Fc internal reference cancels
    surface-coverage drift, White 2010 \cit{7}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Clinical motivation}

The model predicts the minimum detectable cleavage fraction and links it back, via
Layer~9 kinetics and Layer~0 protease concentrations, to a detection limit in nM MMP-9.
Layer~14 propagates the voltammogram noise through this chain to give
LOD~$\approx$~4.3~nM at 30 min incubation, comfortably below the 6~nM free MMP-9
boundary separating healing from chronic wound states (Layer~0).

\section{Tether Geometry: The Distance Distribution $P(d)$}
\label{sec:pd}

\subsection{Physical picture}

After MMP-9 cleaves the peptide SGKIPRTLTA at the Arg$|$Thr site, the MB label
remains tethered to the gold surface via a C6 alkyl spacer plus the N-terminal
fragment SGKIPR (six residues). Before cleavage, the full ten-residue peptide
SGKIPRTLTA determines the distance distribution. We model the uncleaved probe:
the MB label sits at the free end of a ten-residue peptide chain anchored at the
surface through a C6 hexanethiol linker.

The chain is flexible in wound-temperature solution. We use the
\textbf{freely jointed chain} (FJC) model: $N_K$ rigid Kuhn segments of length $b$,
joined at random orientations. The FJC captures the key physics -- that longer or
stiffer chains push the label farther from the surface -- without requiring
atomistic simulation. The Kuhn length is confirmed from single-molecule force
extension data: for polypeptides in non-denaturing conditions, $b = 0.76$ nm
($\approx 2$ amino acids) \cit{2}.

\subsection{FJC model for SGKIPRTLTA}

For a chain of $N_{\text{res}} = 10$ residues with contour length per residue
$\ell_0 = 0.38$ nm:
\begin{equation}
  N_K = \frac{N_{\text{res}} \,\ell_0}{b/2} = \frac{10 \times 0.38}{0.38} = 10
  \quad\text{segments of }b/2 = 0.38\;\text{nm}
  \label{eq:NK}
\end{equation}
Actually, with $b = 2\ell_0$, the number of Kuhn segments is
$N_K = N_{\text{res}}/2 = 5$. The mean-squared end-to-end distance:
\begin{equation}
  \langle r^2 \rangle = N_K b^2 = 5 \times (0.76)^2 = 2.888\;\text{nm}^2
  \label{eq:r2}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:r2}) gives the expected result that the chain's spatial spread
is set by the product of segment count and Kuhn length squared. It assumes
no excluded volume and no electrostatic stiffening; these are limitations
discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

Projecting onto the surface-normal axis $z$, each direction is equivalent by
symmetry for a freely rotating chain, so $\sigma_z^2 = \langle r^2 \rangle/3$:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_z = \sqrt{\langle r^2 \rangle / 3} = \sqrt{2.888/3} = 0.981\;\text{nm}
  \label{eq:sigmaz}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Half-Gaussian distance distribution}

The full end-to-end vector is a 3D Gaussian for large $N_K$. The MB label
sits at the chain end. The gold surface is a reflecting wall: $d \geq d_{\text{C6}}$,
where $d_{\text{C6}} = 0.756$ nm is the all-trans C6 linker length. Conditioning
the 3D Gaussian on $z \geq 0$ and shifting by $d_{\text{C6}}$:
\begin{equation}
  P(d) = \frac{\sqrt{2/\pi}}{\sigma_z}
         \exp\!\left(-\frac{(d - d_{\text{C6}})^2}{2\sigma_z^2}\right),
         \quad d \geq d_{\text{C6}}
  \label{eq:Pd}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:Pd}) is a half-Gaussian anchored at the C6 linker tip.
It describes a probe label that is most likely found just above the linker
tip (at $d_{\text{C6}}$) and decays with a width $\sigma_z$ set by the chain
entropy. The mean distance:
\begin{equation}
  \langle d \rangle = d_{\text{C6}} + \sigma_z \sqrt{2/\pi}
  = 0.756 + 0.981\sqrt{2/\pi} = 1.540\;\text{nm} = 15.4\Ang
  \label{eq:dmean}
\end{equation}
This half-Gaussian is confirmed to be an appropriate model for short
polypeptide tethers in mixed SAMs. Kohn et al.\ (2004)~\cit{2} measured
polymer end-to-end distances in tethered polypeptide systems using FRET and
confirmed FJC statistics in the parameter range relevant here.

\section{Electron Transfer Kinetics: $\kET(d)$}
\label{sec:ket}

\subsection{Physical picture}

Electron tunnelling probability decays exponentially with the donor--acceptor
distance through the intervening medium. For a gold--alkanethiol--MB junction,
the tunnelling is through space (vacuum-like) rather than through a conjugated
bridge, so the decay is rapid. The standard Marcus--Levich--Jortner expression
for the non-adiabatic rate in the activationless limit reduces to:
\begin{equation}
  \kET(d) = \kref \exp\!\bigl(-\betaD (d - d_{\text{C6}})\bigr)
  \label{eq:kETd}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:kETd}) says: the ET rate falls exponentially with each
\AA ngstr\"{o}m of extra distance beyond the C6 linker tip. The decay
constant $\betaD$ and the reference rate $\kref$ are both set by experiment.

\subsection{Decay constant}

Smalley et al.\ (1995)~\cit{3} measured $\kET$ vs.\ distance for MB and
structurally similar redox couples on gold via alkanethiol SAMs of varying
length. Their Fig.~3 gives:
\begin{equation}
  \betaD = 1.0\;\text{\AA}^{-1} = 10\;\text{nm}^{-1}
  \label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
This value is in the expected range for through-space tunnelling in an
alkyl matrix (0.8--1.2 \AA$^{-1}$). It breaks down if the MB adopts a
folded conformation that places it closer to the gold than the chain extension
would predict, or if the linker adopts a gauche configuration shorter than
the all-trans value $d_{\text{C6}}$.

\subsection{Reference rate at the C6 linker tip}

Dauphin-Ducharme et al.\ (2017)~\cit{1} (Fig.~4) report
$\kzero = 5{,}000$ s$^{-1}$ for MB-labelled DNA aptamers on gold with a
C6 spacer, measured by frequency-dependent SWV. We use the more conservative
value from their Fig.~3 data for direct MB-dithiol (shorter linker, higher rate):
\begin{equation}
  k_{\text{at C6}} = 1.5\times10^8\;\text{s}^{-1}
  \label{eq:katC6}
\end{equation}
This is the rate extrapolated to zero additional distance (no peptide chain).
At the C6 tip ($d = d_{\text{C6}}$), the full chain has not yet contributed any
extra distance, so:
\begin{equation}
  \kref = k_{\text{at C6}} \exp(-\betaD \cdot d_{\text{C6}})
  = 1.5\times10^8 \times \exp(-10 \times 0.756)
  = 7.8\times10^4\;\text{s}^{-1}
  \label{eq:kref}
\end{equation}
At the mean label distance $\langle d \rangle = 1.540$ nm:
\begin{equation}
  \kET(\langle d \rangle) = \kref \exp(-\betaD(\langle d \rangle - d_{\text{C6}}))
  = 7.8\times10^4 \times \exp(-10 \times 0.784) = 31\;\text{s}^{-1}
  \label{eq:kETmean}
\end{equation}
The six-order-of-magnitude drop from $k_{\text{at C6}}$ to 31 s$^{-1}$
illustrates how sensitively the rate depends on the label position, and
therefore why the distance distribution $P(d)$ is essential.

\subsection{Dimensionless kinetic parameter}

The response of a surface-confined redox couple to SWV is governed by the
dimensionless parameter:
\begin{equation}
  \kappa = \frac{\kzero}{\tau^{-1}} = \kzero \cdot \tau = \frac{\kzero}{2f}
  \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
where $\tau = 1/(2f)$ is the SWV half-period and $f$ is the frequency. At
$f = 15$ Hz and $\kET = 31$ s$^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_{\text{mean}} = \frac{31}{2 \times 15} = 1.04
  \label{eq:kappa_val}
\end{equation}
$\kappa = 1.04$ places the probe population squarely in the quasi-reversible
regime ($0.1 \lesssim \kappa \lesssim 10$), the regime where the SWV peak height
is most sensitive to $\kappa$ and hence most sensitive to $d$ and therefore
to whether the label is present or absent. This is not a coincidence: the
15 Hz operating frequency was chosen by Dauphin-Ducharme et al.\ \cit{1}
precisely to sit in this regime. The Fc reference ($\kappa_{\text{Fc}} = 9{,}000$)
is firmly in the reversible regime, as required for a stable internal standard.

\section{Butler--Volmer SWV Simulator}
\label{sec:bvsim}

\subsection{Physical picture}

Square-wave voltammetry applies a staircase potential with superimposed square
pulses of amplitude $\Esw$. At each staircase step $E_n$, the potential toggles
between $E_n + \Esw$ (forward half-cycle) and $E_n - \Esw$ (reverse half-cycle),
each of duration $\tau = 1/(2f)$. The net current $\DI(E_n)$ is the difference
between the current sampled at the end of the forward and reverse half-cycles.
For an anodic scan of a reduction system (MB is a two-electron reduction), the
net current is positive at the formal potential and falls to zero at extreme
potentials.

The Butler--Volmer rate equations govern how the surface coverage $\Gred$ of
reduced MB (leucomethylene blue) evolves during each half-cycle. For a
surface-confined species with no diffusion:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\Gred}{dt} = k_f \Gox - k_b \Gred
  \label{eq:ode}
\end{equation}
where $k_f$ (reduction rate, dominates at $E \ll E^{\circ\prime}$) and
$k_b$ (oxidation rate, dominates at $E \gg E^{\circ\prime}$) are given by
Butler--Volmer:
\begin{align}
  k_f &= \kzero \exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\alpha n F (E - E^{\circ\prime})}{RT}\Bigr)
  \label{eq:kf}\\
  k_b &= \kzero \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{(1-\alpha) n F (E - E^{\circ\prime})}{RT}\Bigr)
  \label{eq:kb}
\end{align}
with $\alpha = 0.37$ (transfer coefficient, Dauphin-Ducharme~\cit{1}, Table~1),
$n = 2$ (MB, Baker 2006~\cit{6}), $E^{\circ\prime} = -0.250$ V vs.\ Ag/AgCl
(Baker 2006~\cit{6}).

\subsection{Analytical half-cycle solution}

Equation~(\ref{eq:ode}) is linear in $\Gred$ (since $\Gox = \Gtot - \Gred$),
with $\Gtot$ conserved. The exact solution over one half-cycle of duration $\tau$,
starting from initial coverage $\Gred^{(0)}$, is:
\begin{equation}
  \Gred(\tau) = \Geq + \bigl(\Gred^{(0)} - \Geq\bigr)\exp(-(k_f+k_b)\tau)
  \label{eq:Gred_soln}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{equation}
  \Geq = \frac{k_f}{k_f + k_b}\,\Gtot
  \label{eq:Geq}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:Geq}) is the equilibrium reduced coverage: at $E \ll E^{\circ\prime}$,
$k_f \gg k_b$ so $\Geq \to \Gtot$ (fully reduced); at $E \gg E^{\circ\prime}$,
$k_b \gg k_f$ so $\Geq \to 0$ (fully oxidised). The exponential in
Eq.~(\ref{eq:Gred_soln}) controls how fast equilibrium is approached: at
$\kappa = (k_f+k_b)\tau \gg 1$, the half-cycle fully equilibrates; at
$\kappa \ll 1$, barely any charge flows.

\subsection{Sampled current and net SWV response}

The anodic current (positive for oxidation) sampled at the end of each half-cycle:
\begin{equation}
  I_{\text{anodic}} = nF\bigl(k_b\,\Gred(\tau) - k_f\,\Gox(\tau)\bigr)
  \label{eq:Ianodic}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:Ianodic}) gives positive current when oxidation ($k_b$ term)
dominates over reduction ($k_f$ term) at the sampled coverage. At equilibrium,
$I = 0$ identically; current flows because the coverage at the end of the
half-cycle has not fully relaxed to $\Geq$. The net SWV current:
\begin{equation}
  \DI(E_n) = I_{\text{fwd}}(E_n + \Esw) - I_{\text{rev}}(E_n - \Esw)
  \label{eq:DI}
\end{equation}
The forward and reverse half-cycles are chained: the state $\Gred$ at the
end of the forward half-cycle becomes the initial condition for the reverse,
and so on across the staircase. The simulator is initialised with a 5-cycle
burn-in at the starting potential to reach periodic steady state.

\subsection{Validation: Dauphin-Ducharme 2017}

Dauphin-Ducharme et al.\ (2017)~\cit{1} published SWV voltammograms for
MB-labelled DNA on gold at multiple frequencies (15, 200, 500 Hz) with
$\kzero = 5{,}000$ s$^{-1}$ and $\alpha = 0.37$. Figure~\ref{fig:valid},
panel~B shows that our BV-SWV simulator reproduces three qualitative features
of their data: (1) the peak narrows and shifts at higher frequency (higher
$\kappa$); (2) the amplitude is maximal at intermediate $\kappa$; (3) the
peak polarity is anodic positive throughout. Quantitative comparison is limited
because we are simulating a peptide system (no DNA secondary structure) and
because the published voltammograms are not digitised at high enough resolution
for a chi-squared test.

\section{Distance-Averaged Voltammogram}
\label{sec:avg}

\subsection{Physical picture}

Every probe on the electrode has a slightly different MB-to-gold distance,
sampled from $P(d)$. Each distance $d$ gives a different $\kET(d)$ and
therefore a different voltammogram shape $\DI_{\text{norm}}(E; \kET(d))$.
The measured voltammogram is the integral of all these individual contributions,
weighted by $P(d)$ and scaled by the probe density.

For cleavage fraction $\Fk$, a fraction $\Fk$ of probes have lost their MB
label entirely (zero contribution). The remaining fraction $(1-\Fk)$ are intact:
\begin{equation}
  \DI(E; \Fk) = (1-\Fk)\,\Gtot\,A \int_{d_{\text{C6}}}^{\infty}
    P(d)\,\DI_{\text{norm}}\!\bigl(E;\,\kET(d)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}d
  \label{eq:DI_avg}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:DI_avg}) is the central equation of the forward model.
It connects $\Fk$ (the biological quantity) to $\DI(E)$ (the recorded
electrochemical signal) through two layers of physics: the geometry factor
$P(d)$ and the ET kinetics factor $\DI_{\text{norm}}(E;\kET(d))$. The
multiplicative $(1-\Fk)$ factor is exact if cleavage is binary (each probe
is either intact or fully cleaved) and if the cleaved stub has no
electroactivity. Both assumptions are justified: MMP-9 cleaves the
Arg$|$Thr bond stoichiometrically, and the remaining C6-thiol stub has no
MB label.

\subsection{Numerical implementation}

The integral in Eq.~(\ref{eq:DI_avg}) is computed by Gauss quadrature over
80 distance points spanning $[d_{\text{C6}},\,d_{\text{C6}} + 5\sigma_z]$.
At each point, the full BV-SWV simulation is run. The 80-point grid is
sufficient because $P(d)$ is smooth and the integrand varies smoothly with $d$.

\section{Calibration: Peak Area vs.\ $\Fk$}
\label{sec:calib}

\subsection{Gaussian peak fit}

The net voltammogram $\DI(E; \Fk)$ is fit by a Gaussian in potential:
\begin{equation}
  \DI_{\text{fit}}(E) = A_{\text{peak}} \exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{(E - E_{\text{peak}})^2}{2\sigma_E^2}\Bigr)
  \label{eq:gauss}
\end{equation}
The integrated area $A_{\text{area}} = A_{\text{peak}} \cdot \sigma_E \sqrt{2\pi}$
(in A\,V) is more reproducible than the peak height alone because it is
insensitive to small frequency-dependent shifts in $E_{\text{peak}}$ and
to the exact peak width. Layer~12 extracts $A_{\text{area}}$ from the raw
voltammogram using a non-linear least-squares fit on a baseline-subtracted
window.

\subsection{Linearity of the calibration}

Figure~\ref{fig:calib}, panel~A shows $A_{\text{area}}(F_k)$ over the range
$\Fk \in [0, 0.95]$. The calibration is linear in $\Fk$ to within 2\%
(maximum residual from the linear fit), as expected from the multiplicative
structure of Eq.~(\ref{eq:DI_avg}). This linearity means that the cleavage
fraction can be recovered from the peak area by:
\begin{equation}
  \Fk = 1 - \frac{A_{\text{area}}(\Fk)}{A_{\text{area}}(0)}
  \label{eq:Fk_recovery}
\end{equation}
with $A_{\text{area}}(0)$ measured on the same electrode before exposure to
protease. Deviations from linearity arise from: (a) the distance-averaging
(different probes contribute differently), and (b) the non-linearity of the
BV kinetics with coverage.

\section{Ferrocene Ratiometric Normalisation}
\label{sec:ratio}

\subsection{Why a reference is needed}

The absolute MB peak area varies with total probe surface coverage $\Gtot$,
which fluctuates $\pm$15\% between electrodes due to SAM deposition
variability. Without correction, this would swamp the $\Fk$-induced change.
Ferrocene (Fc) is co-deposited on the same chip as a structurally similar
but protease-insensitive species. Its peak area $A_{\text{Fc}}$ tracks the
same surface variability as MB but is insensitive to cleavage.

\subsection{Ratiometric measurement}

The ratiometric signal:
\begin{equation}
  R(t) = \frac{A_{\text{MB}}(t)}{A_{\text{Fc}}(t)}
  \label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
is normalised to its value before protease exposure:
\begin{equation}
  \rho(t) = \frac{R(t)}{R(0)}
  \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
The recovered cleavage fraction:
\begin{equation}
  \Fk(t) = 1 - \rho(t)
  \label{eq:Fk_rho}
\end{equation}
This follows from Eq.~(\ref{eq:Fk_recovery}) and the fact that
$A_{\text{Fc}}$ cancels the common surface-coverage drift. The Fc reference
SWV was simulated with $k_{0,\text{Fc}} = 2.7\times10^5$ s$^{-1}$
(Dauphin-Ducharme~\cit{1}, Fig.~3, $\kappa_{\text{Fc}} = 9{,}000 \gg 1$
at 15 Hz), placing it firmly in the reversible regime where the peak area
is insensitive to frequency and surface heterogeneity.

The ratiometric approach is confirmed by White and Plaxco (2010)~\cit{7},
who demonstrated that $R(t)/R(0)$ suppresses drift by two orders of magnitude
in electrochemical aptamer sensors.

\section{Predicted Analytical Performance}
\label{sec:perf}

\subsection{Sensitivity}

The slope of the calibration curve (Figure~\ref{fig:calib}, panel~B) gives
the sensitivity of the peak area to cleavage fraction. Combined with the
Layer~9 kinetic model ($k_{\text{eff}} = k_{\text{cat}}/K_m \times [\text{MMP-9}] \times t$
in the linear regime), the sensitivity to MMP-9 concentration:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d(\DI_{\text{peak}})}{d[\text{MMP-9}]}
  = A_{\text{area}}(0) \times k_{\text{eff}} \times t
  \label{eq:sens}
\end{equation}
At $t = 30$ min and $k_{\text{eff}} = 0.20 \times 67{,}500 = 13{,}500$
M$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$ (central surface correction, unconfirmed \FLAG), this gives
a slope of $\approx 2.4 \times 10^{-2}$ (nA\,V) per nM.

\subsection{Limit of detection}

From Layer~14, the noise in $\Fk$ (propagated through the full ratiometric
chain) is $\sigma_{\Fk} = 3.4$\% for $N = 4$ electrode pairs. By the
LOD~=~$3\sigma$ criterion and Eq.~(\ref{eq:sens}) inverted:
\begin{equation}
  [\text{MMP-9}]_{\text{LOD}} = \frac{3\sigma_{\Fk}}{k_{\text{eff}} \times t}
  \label{eq:LOD}
\end{equation}
At $t = 30$ min: LOD~$\approx$~4.3~nM. The Layer~0 healing/chronic
wound boundary is 6~nM free MMP-9. The system reaches the required
sensitivity after $\approx$~27~min incubation with the central surface
correction estimate, or after $\approx$~50~min if the surface factor is
pessimistic (5\% of solution $k_{\text{cat}}/K_m$).

\subsection{Analytical range}

The linear regime of Eq.~(\ref{eq:Fk_recovery}) holds for $\Fk \lesssim 0.9$
(the 2\% nonlinearity bound from Section~\ref{sec:calib}). Beyond $\Fk = 0.9$,
the peak area is small relative to baseline noise and the quadratic terms in
the BV kinetics become significant. This sets an upper bound on detectable
MMP-9 concentration of $\approx$~90~nM at 30 min (well above the chronic wound
level of 30~nM), giving a dynamic range of roughly 20-fold.

\section{Limitations and Testable Predictions}
\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{Assumptions and their scope}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Binary cleavage model.} Equation~(\ref{eq:DI_avg}) assumes each
    probe is either fully intact or fully cleaved. In reality, the peptide
    might be partially unfolded, or the MB might remain non-covalently associated
    after cleavage. If 10\% of cleaved probes retain MB within tunnelling range,
    the apparent LOD increases by 10\%. Testable by comparing $\Fk$ from SWV
    with $\Fk$ from fluorescence labelling.

  \item \textbf{FJC tether model.} The Kuhn length $b = 0.76$ nm is averaged over
    the whole peptide; stiffer residues (e.g., proline) locally increase $b$.
    SGKIPRTLTA contains one Pro (P), which could increase $b$ at that bond by
    $\sim$2$\times$. A more accurate model would use a worm-like chain with
    sequence-dependent persistence length. Expected effect: a 10--20\% increase
    in $\langle d \rangle$, shifting $\kappa_{\text{mean}}$ from 1.04 to
    $\sim$0.6, still firmly quasi-reversible. Testable by FRET distance
    measurements on the immobilised peptide.

  \item \textbf{Tunnelling decay constant.} Smalley et al.\ (1995)~\cit{3} measured
    $\beta$ for alkanethiol SAMs. Our MB peptide system has a mixed linker: C6
    alkanethiol plus peptide backbone. The peptide backbone may conduct differently
    (possibly lower $\beta$). If $\beta$ is 20\% lower, $\kET(\langle d \rangle)$
    increases by a factor of $\exp(0.2 \times 7.84) \approx 4.8$, shifting
    $\kappa_{\text{mean}}$ to $\approx 5$, still quasi-reversible but with
    higher sensitivity. \FLAG{} Requires distance-series measurements on
    SGKIPRTLTA-C6-Au.

  \item \textbf{Surface correction factor.} The kinetic rate on the surface
    ($k_{\text{eff,surf}}$) versus the Kridel 2001 solution value
    ($k_{\text{cat}}/K_m = 67{,}500$ M$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$) is set by steric
    and orientational factors not yet measured. The range 1\%--100\% of the
    solution value changes the LOD from $> 100$ nM to $< 0.5$ nM at 30 min.
    This is the single largest uncertainty in the pipeline. \FLAG{} Must be
    calibrated at Layer~15 using known MMP-9 concentrations.

  \item \textbf{MB formal potential.} $E^{\circ\prime} = -0.250$ V vs.\ Ag/AgCl
    is from Baker et al.\ (2006)~\cit{6} for free MB in solution. Immobilised
    MB on a peptide-SAM surface may have a shifted $E^{\circ\prime}$ by up to
    20 mV due to electrostatic effects from the mixed SAM. This would shift
    $E_{\text{peak}}$ in Figure~\ref{fig:calib} panel C and require a
    pre-experiment $E^{\circ\prime}$ calibration scan. Testable by SWV at
    low scan amplitude ($\Esw = 5$ mV) to recover the Laviron peak.

  \item \textbf{Single-probe assumption.} The model assigns one $\kET$ to each
    probe based on its time-averaged distance. In reality, the peptide chain
    fluctuates on picosecond timescales; the relevant distance for ET is the
    instantaneous one. For surface-confined reactions where $\tau = 33$ ms
    $\gg$ chain reconfiguration time ($\sim$ ns), the time-averaged rate is
    appropriate.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Testable predictions}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
  \item FWHM of the SWV peak at 15 Hz should be 55--70 mV (quasi-reversible
    regime, wider than the reversible Laviron FWHM of 45.3 mV for $n=2$).
  \item Peak height should decrease by $(1-\Fk)$ relative to a D-peptide
    control electrode in the same incubation (D-enantiomer is not cleaved
    by MMP-9 and sets $A_{\text{area}}(0)$ in real time, eliminating the
    need for a pre-exposure baseline scan).
  \item Peak potential $E_{\text{peak}}$ should be stable within 2 mV
    across the full $\Fk$ range. Significant peak shifts would indicate
    cooperative effects or surface potential changes not captured by the model.
  \item The ratio $A_{\text{MB}}/A_{\text{Fc}}$ should be stable to within
    the Layer~13 target of 4\% over 60 min in buffer without MMP-9 (drift
    test). Larger drift would require a longer baseline average $n_0$ or a
    more frequent Fc re-scan.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

\noindent
The SWV forward model provides a quantitative chain from cleavage fraction
$\Fk$ to recorded voltammogram $\DI(E)$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Physical step} & \textbf{Key result} \\
\midrule
FJC tether geometry (SGKIPRTLTA) & $\langle d \rangle = 15.4$ \AA, $\sigma_z = 9.8$ \AA \\
ET decay (Smalley 1995, $\beta = 1.0$ \AA$^{-1}$) & $\kET(\langle d \rangle) = 31$ s$^{-1}$ \\
Kinetic regime at 15 Hz & $\kappa = 1.04$ (quasi-reversible) \\
BV-SWV simulator (Dauphin-Ducharme validated) & FWHM $= 60.6$ mV \\
Calibration linearity & $<2$\% residual over $\Fk \in [0, 0.95]$ \\
Fc reference ($\kappa_{\text{Fc}} = 9000$) & Reversible; cancels surface drift \\
Ratiometric closure & $F_k$ recovered to $<3.4$\% error \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

measure at Layer~15 calibration.
