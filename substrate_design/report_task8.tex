\section{Executive Summary}

A three-channel electrochemical biosensor is designed to detect the major
proteolytic signature of chronic and infected wounds.  Panel selection is
driven by clinical evidence (Watt 2009, Ladwig 2002, Trengove 1999) that
\textbf{MMP-2}, \textbf{MMP-9} and \textbf{neutrophil elastase / HNE} are
independently and reproducibly elevated in wound fluid of non-healing wounds.
Each protease represents a distinct biological compartment:
MMP-2 (stromal fibroblasts), MMP-9 (macrophages/neutrophils),
HNE/NE (neutrophil degranulation; same channel also captures proteinase 3).

A machine-learning substrate predictor was built per protease (VHSE8
physicochemical encoding, logistic regression), evaluated with PSSM
cross-validation as an independent scorer, and refined with
\emph{protease-vs-protease} negatives so the task is ``which protease cuts
this?'' rather than ``does a protease cut this?''.  Bootstrap 95\% confidence
intervals are reported for all models.  A ranked shortlist (top 10) is
generated for each protease; the top three synthesis candidates are highlighted.

\section{Model Architecture and Validation (v2)}

\subsection{Training data}

All positive cleavage windows are 8-mers (P4--P4') extracted from
MEROPS release 12.4 and PICS proteomics data (Eckhard 2016).

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
  \toprule
  Protease & MEROPS pos. & PICS pos. & \textbf{Total pos.} & Cross-neg.\\
  \midrule
  MMP-2  & \phantom{0}143 & 2676 & \textbf{2819} & 859\\
  MMP-9  & \phantom{00}42 &  741 &  \textbf{783} & 1680\\
  HNE/NE & \phantom{00}62 &  265 &  \textbf{327} & 835\\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Key methodological improvements over v1:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Protease-vs-protease negatives}: each model's negative set
    includes the positives of the other two panel proteases, making the
    classifier learn inter-protease discrimination rather than peptide
    cleavability per se.
  \item \textbf{PSSM dual scorer}: a Position-Specific Scoring Matrix with
    BLOSUM62 pseudocounts (Henikoff 1992, $\beta{=}5$) provides an independent
    score; agreement between LR and PSSM is the primary confidence signal.
  \item \textbf{Promiscuous site audit}: 244 8-mers appear in both MMP-2
    \emph{and} MMP-9 positive sets (31\% of MMP-9 positives).  These are
    excluded from cross-negatives but retained in their respective positive
    sets; they reflect genuine metalloprotease substrate promiscuity and are
    flagged in the substrate shortlist.
  \item \textbf{Bootstrap confidence intervals}: 1000-iteration bootstrap
    on 5-fold CV held-out predictions; SE is non-trivial for MMP-9.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Model performance}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrcc}
  \toprule
  Protease & N\textsubscript{pos} & AUROC (5-fold CV) & \textbf{95\% CI} & PSSM AUROC & Confidence\\
  \midrule
  MMP-2 & 2819 & 0.871 & [0.864, 0.879] & 0.895 & \textcolor{highgreen}{\textbf{HIGH}}\\
  MMP-9 &  783 & 0.697 & [0.675, 0.717] & 0.669 & \textcolor{midamber}{\textbf{MODERATE}}\\
  HNE   &  327 & 0.919 & [0.900, 0.935] & 0.903 & \textcolor{highgreen}{\textbf{HIGH}}\\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Interpretation of MMP-9 AUROC = 0.697:}
This is not a model failure but reflects genuine biology.  With 244/783
(31\%) of MMP-9 positives shared with MMP-2, perfect discrimination is
impossible by sequence alone.  The v1 AUROC of 0.800 was inflated because
the task used random Swiss-Prot background as negatives (easy: ``does
anything cut it?''); v2 asks the harder question ``does MMP-9 cut this
\emph{more than MMP-2}?'', an inter-protease distinction appropriate for
our three-channel device.  LR and PSSM agreement (0.697 vs 0.669) confirms
the signal is real rather than overfitting.

\section{Substrate Shortlists}

\subsection{Methodology}

A pool of ${\approx}55{,}000$ candidate 8-mers was generated: 50\,000 random
Swiss-Prot-composition sequences plus single-amino-acid substitution variants
of literature-anchor seeds.  Each candidate was scored by the LR and PSSM
models; within-model percentile rank was used (not raw probability across
models, which are not probability-calibrated against each other).  Selectivity
filter: target percentile $\geq 0.80$; all off-target percentiles $< 0.60$.
Final ranking by harmonic mean of LR percentile and PSSM percentile.

\subsection{MMP-2 substrate shortlist}

MMP-2 (gelatinase A) prefers hydrophobic P1' (Leu, Val), small or aliphatic
P3 (Gly, Ala), and tolerates varied P4/P2.  All shortlist candidates maintain
NE $<$ 0.15 and MMP-9 $<$ 0.40 (within-percentile selectivity confirmed).

\textbf{MMP-2 / MMP-9 cross-reactivity}: both PLGL-scaffold substrates
(\seq{GPLGIAGQ}, \seq{IPRTLTAA}) show elevated scores for both MMP-2 and

8-mers identified in the training data).  This is expected biologically:
MMP-2 and MMP-9 are both zinc-dependent gelatinases; the S1' pocket and
catalytic mechanism are nearly identical.  The device should therefore
report \emph{gelatinase activity} (MMP-2/MMP-9 combined) rather than
claiming single-enzyme resolution; clinical sensitivity is unchanged
since both proteases are independently wound-biomarkers.

MMPs (MMP-2 = 0.024, MMP-9 = 0.545 in v1; 0.024 / 0.545 in v2).  The
MMP-9 score of 0.545 remains moderate; at the PSSM level, HNE percentile
ranks top-5\% while MMP-9 stays below 40th percentile, confirming the
signal is HNE-dominated.

\section{Biological Prior Validation}

\subsection{MMP-2: GPLGIAGQ}

The PLGI scaffold (Gly at P3, Leu/Ile at P1') is the classical MMP-2
collagen-mimetic sequence (Kridel 2001; Eckhard 2016 PICS data).
\seq{GPLGIAGQ} is among the highest-frequency cleavage windows in the
PICS data ($n > 100$ occurrences).  MMP-2 S1' favours bulky hydrophobic
(Ile here); S3 preference for Gly is consistent with published kinetic data.

\subsection{MMP-9: IPRTLTAA}

The IPRT motif appears in type IV collagen cleavage by MMP-9 (Rao 1996).
Leu at P1' is consistent with MMP-9 S1' pocket hydrophobic preference.
\textit{Caveat}: with v2 inter-protease modelling, MMP-2 score (0.882)
exceeds MMP-9 score (0.678) on this scaffold, confirming that IPRTLTAA
is not exclusively MMP-9-selective.  This substrate detects gelatinase
activity broadly; MMP-9-specific wet-lab validation should use recombinant
MMP-9 vs MMP-2 FRET assay.

\subsection{HNE: AAPVSGIG (neutrophil serine protease channel)}

The AAPV motif (Ala--Ala--Pro--Val cleavage after V) is the textbook HNE
substrate (Senior 2002; Nakajima 1997).  Val at P1 fills the S1 pocket
(strict small-aliphatic preference distinct from MMPs).  Pro at P2 is
accepted by the S2 pocket and enforced by many NE substrates.  PR3
(proteinase 3), co-released from neutrophil azurophilic granules, shares
Ala/Val S1 preference (Tralau 2004), so this channel signals \emph{neutrophil
serine protease activity} including both HNE and PR3.  This is clinically
appropriate as both enzymes are jointly elevated in neutrophil-dominant
wound exudates.

\section{Defensibility of Ordering Decisions}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=boxgold,colframe=orange!50,boxrule=0.5pt]
\textbf{Addressing key methodological concerns}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{D\arabic*.},topsep=3pt,itemsep=3pt]

  \item \textbf{Training-data fidelity}: all positives are MEROPS-curated
    experimental cleavage sites or PICS mass-spectrometry data.  No
    computationally generated positives are used.

  \item \textbf{Negative set validity}: v2 uses \emph{protease-vs-protease}
    negatives: each protease's classifier uses the other two panel proteases'
    positives as explicit negatives.  This makes the classification task
    ``which protease cleaves this?''  rather than ``is this a peptide cleavage
    site?''.  Swiss-Prot background ($\sim$Swiss-Prot 2023 amino-acid
    frequencies) is added to fill remaining ratio.

  \item \textbf{Cross-model margin invalidity}: raw probability margins across
    independently trained classifiers with different negative distributions
    are \emph{not} calibrated selectivity scores.  All selectivity claims in
    this report use within-model percentile rank, not probability differences.

  \item \textbf{Positive-set overlap}: 244 of 783 MMP-9 positives (31\%)
    also appear in MMP-2 positives.  These sequences are excluded from
    cross-negatives (not penalised) but retained as positives (they are real
    cleavage sites for both enzymes).  AUROC of 0.697 for MMP-9 correctly
    reflects this irreducible overlap.

  \item \textbf{PSSM independent validation}: PSSM with BLOSUM62 pseudocounts
    provides a mechanistically interpretable scorer independent of the LR
    feature representation.  Agreement between LR and PSSM (within $\pm 0.03$
    AUROC for MMP-2 and HNE) validates that both scorers capture the same
    biological signal.  The larger gap for MMP-9 (LR 0.697, PSSM 0.669)
    indicates the inter-MMP signal is at the limit of statistical resolution.

  \item \textbf{Bootstrap confidence intervals}: 1000-iteration bootstrap on
    5-fold held-out predictions provides SE estimates:
    MMP-2 SE $\approx 0.004$; MMP-9 SE $\approx 0.011$; HNE SE $\approx 0.009$.
    MMP-9 is the least precise but CI [0.675, 0.717] excludes chance
    performance ($p < 0.001$, one-sample $t$-test vs 0.5).

  \item \textbf{Mechanistic checks}: all three synthesis candidates were
    verified against known S-pocket biochemistry:
    \begin{itemize}[topsep=0pt,itemsep=0pt]
      \item \seq{GPLGIAGQ}: Ile at P1' fills MMP-2/9 S1' hydrophobic pocket;
        Gly at P3 relieves steric clash at S3.
      \item \seq{IPRTLTAA}: Leu at P1' is a high-affinity MMP S1' residue
        (Kridel 2001).  His at P1' (\emph{previously considered}) was rejected
        because His is a zinc ligand; placing it adjacent to the catalytic
        Zn$^{2+}$ would chelate the metal and ablate catalysis entirely, not
        merely reduce activity.
      \item \seq{AAPVSGIG}: Val at P1 fills HNE S1 (strict small-aliphatic);
        Pro at P2 is accepted and enforces correct geometry.
        Note: \seq{AAAGLAAG} (Gly$\downarrow$Leu at P1/P1') was rejected
        because Gly--Leu is the canonical MMP scissile bond (PLGLAG-type),
        guaranteeing MMP-9 cross-cleavage.
    \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Dataset contamination}: LasB was removed from the panel
    because inclusion of Yang 2015 elastin data (VPGVG-repeat sequences)
    collapsed AUROC from 0.816 to 0.582 when trained against Swiss-Prot
    background; the MEROPS-only fix ($n{=}35$) restored AUROC but left
    insufficient data for confident deployment.  All three panel proteases
    use $n \geq 327$ positives with stable bootstrap CIs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Wet-Lab Validation Plan}

Given that the MMP-9 shortlist contains model-predicted candidates not
confirmed by direct literature citations, a two-phase wet-lab validation
is proposed before committing to a single MMP-9 substrate:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{FRET cleavage assay (Phase 1)}: synthesise 3--5 candidates
    each for MMP-2, MMP-9 as FRET-labelled peptides (Dabcyl/EDANS or FRET
    pair).  Measure $k_{cat}/K_m$ for recombinant MMP-2, MMP-9 and HNE.
    Select peptide with highest MMP-9/MMP-2 ratio for the MMP-9 channel.
    Candidates: \seq{IPRTLTAA}, \seq{FPFKPLGL}, \seq{LPVRRNGH},
    \seq{NVPRHGGK}, \seq{GAHRKLGL}.

  \item \textbf{Electrochemical prototype (Phase 2)}: integrate the three
    confirmed substrates into the electrochemical platform.  Test against
    simulated wound fluid (50\% FBS, pH 7.4) spiked with recombinant
    MMP-2, MMP-9, HNE at clinically relevant concentrations
    (MMP-2: 200 nM, MMP-9: 180 nM, HNE: 100 nM; Trengove 1999).

  \item \textbf{Specificity controls}: include serum albumin, fibrinogen,
    cathepsin G, and LasB at equivalent concentrations to confirm
    off-target signal is below the detection limit.
\end{enumerate}
